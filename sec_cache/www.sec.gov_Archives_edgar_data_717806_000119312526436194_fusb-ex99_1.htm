CEO Commentary

 

“We are pleased to report higher earnings and continued expansion of net interest margin during the third quarter,” stated James F. House, President and CEO of the Company. “Net income increased by 11.8% and diluted earnings per share ("EPS") by 16.1% compared to the second quarter. Year-to-date net income increased by 46.5% and diluted earnings per share by 56.3% compared to the same period last year. The improvement in net income along with a successful share repurchase campaign during 2026, contributed to strong earnings per share improvement. Our net interest margin improved to 3.66% for the third quarter, while nonperforming assets and net charge-offs as a percentage of average loans declined from the second quarter. Growth in our indirect consumer portfolio continued, while expected project payoffs in multi-family and construction lending contributed to a decrease in total loans. We remain focused on disciplined pricing and credit quality while maintaining flexibility to respond to opportunities in our markets,” continued Mr. House.

Total loans decreased by $15.6 million, or 1.8%, to $845.0 million during 3Q2026. Expected project payoffs contributed to declines of $20.7 million in multi-family residential loans and $5.3 million in construction, land development and other land loans. Commercial and industrial loans decreased by $3.3 million. These decreases were partially offset by growth in indirect consumer loans and modest increases in the other portfolio categories. While the Company has experienced substantial payoffs of real estate loans during 2026 (including construction, multi-family and commercial real estate), the commercial lending team is strategically focused on building and maintaining commercial lending relationships that are expected to assist future loan growth. During the nine months ended September 30, 2026, total loans decreased by $8.0 million, or 0.9%. Average total loans for the nine-month period were $854.3 million, an increase of $2.7 million, or 0.3%, compared to the corresponding period of 2025.

 

The Company’s indirect lending platform focuses on borrowers at the higher end of the credit spectrum, as reflected in the portfolio’s weighted average credit score at the time of funding of 785 as of September 30, 2026. Indirect consumer loans increased by $13.1 million, or 3.2%, during 3Q2026 and by $35.8 million, or 9.4%, during the nine months ended September 30, 2026. Collateral primarily consists of boats, recreational vehicles, campers, horse trailers and cargo trailers.

 

The average yield on total loans was 5.98% in 3Q2026, compared to 5.90% in 2Q2026 and 6.10% in 3Q2025. For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, the average yield on loans was 5.94%, compared to 6.07% for the corresponding period of 2025.

Compared to December 31, 2025, total deposits decreased by $12.9 million, or 1.3%, reflecting a $21.9 million decrease in wholesale brokered deposits, partially offset by growth in non-wholesale deposits. Cash and Investment Securities – As of September 30, 2026, the Company held cash, federal funds sold and securities purchased under reverse repurchase agreements totaling $66.4 million, or 5.7% of total assets, compared to $78.4 million, or 6.8% of total assets, as of December 31, 2025. Net Interest Income and Margin – Net interest income totaled $9.9 million in 3Q2026, reflecting an increase of $350 thousand, or 3.7%, compared to 2Q2026 and an increase of $188 thousand, or 1.9%, compared to 3Q2025. Net interest margin increased to 3.66% in 3Q2026, compared to 3.56% in 2Q2026 and 3.60% in 3Q2025. The sequential improvement reflected higher yields on loans and investment securities, while the average rate on total deposits remained relatively stable. Compared to 3Q2025, lower deposit costs and a higher yield on investment securities more than offset a lower loan yield. For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, net interest income totaled $28.6 million, reflecting an increase of $530 thousand, or 1.9%, compared to the corresponding period of 2025. Net interest margin was 3.53% for the nine-month period, compared to 3.57% for the corresponding period of 2025, reflecting lower loan yields that were partially offset by higher investment securities yields and lower deposit costs.

 

Asset Quality – Nonperforming assets, including loans in non-accrual status, other real estate owned and repossessed assets, totaled $1.7 million as of September 30, 2026, compared to $1.9 million as of June 30, 2026 and $1.6 million as of December 31, 2025. As a percentage of total assets, nonperforming assets represented 0.14% of total assets as of September 30, 2026, compared to 0.17% as of June 30, 2026 and 0.14% as of December 31, 2025. Annualized net charge-offs as a percentage of average loans were 0.22% in 3Q2026, compared to 0.30% in 2Q2026 and 0.61% in 3Q2025. For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, annualized net charge-offs as a percentage of average loans were 0.25%, compared to 0.52% for the corresponding period of 2025. The year-over-year improvement reflected, in part, the absence of a $2.2 million charge-off of an individually evaluated commercial loan recorded in 2025.

 

Provision for Credit Losses – During 3Q2026, the Company recorded a provision for credit losses totaling $586 thousand, compared to $939 thousand in 2Q2026 and $566 thousand in 3Q2025. For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, the provision for credit losses totaled $1.8 million, compared to $3.8 million for the corresponding period of 2025. The year-over-year decrease resulted primarily from provisioning for specifically identified commercial loans in 2025, which were resolved by the end of 2025 and, accordingly, the same level of provisioning was not repeated during the nine months ended September 30, 2026. The Company’s ACL on loans and leases totaled $11.0 million, or 1.30% of total loans, as of September 30, 2026, compared to $10.9 million, or 1.26%, as of June 30, 2026 and $10.7 million, or 1.25%, as of December 31, 2025. The increase in the ACL as a percentage of total loans during both the three- and nine-month periods ended September 30, 2026 reflected changes in loan portfolio composition, including more prominent growth in indirect consumer loans, which require higher credit allowances than other loan categories, as well as updated economic forecast data released in 3Q2026. While management believes that the ACL is adequate to absorb credit losses within the Company’s loan portfolio, inherent uncertainty exists regarding the ultimate impact of geopolitical and economic matters, including prospective inflation, unemployment levels, tariffs and consumer affordability.

 

Pre-tax Pre-provision Net Revenue (“PPNR”) – PPNR totaled $3.1 million in 3Q2026, compared to $3.2 million in 2Q2026 and $3.1 million in 3Q2025. For the nine months ended September 30, 2026, PPNR totaled $9.0 million, or 1.05% (annualized) of average assets, compared to $8.8 million, or 1.06% (annualized) of average assets, for the corresponding period of 2025. PPNR is a non-GAAP financial measure. Refer to the Non-GAAP Financial Measures section for a reconciliation of PPNR to net income.

Tangible Balances and Measures

In addition to capital ratios defined by GAAP and banking regulators, the Company utilizes various tangible common equity measures when evaluating capital utilization and adequacy. These measures, which are presented in the financial tables in this press release, may also include calculations of tangible assets. As defined by the Company, tangible common equity represents shareholders’ equity less goodwill and identifiable intangible assets, while tangible assets represent total assets less goodwill and identifiable intangible assets.

Management believes that the measures of tangible equity are important because they reflect the level of capital available to withstand unexpected market conditions. In addition, presentation of these measures allows readers to compare certain aspects of the Company’s capitalization to other organizations. In management’s experience, many stock analysts use tangible common equity measures in conjunction with more traditional bank capital ratios to compare capital adequacy of banking organizations with significant amounts of goodwill or other intangible assets that typically result from the use of the purchase accounting method in accounting for mergers and acquisitions.

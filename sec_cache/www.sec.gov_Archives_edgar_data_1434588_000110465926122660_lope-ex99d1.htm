GRAND CANYON EDUCATION, INC. REPORTS

THIRD QUARTER 2026 RESULTS

​

PHOENIX, AZ, November 4, 2026—Grand Canyon Education, Inc. (NASDAQ: LOPE), (“GCE” or the “Company”), is a publicly traded education services company that currently provides services to 20 university partners. GCE provides a full array of support services in the post-secondary education sector and has developed significant technological solutions, infrastructure and operational processes to provide superior services in these areas on a large scale. GCE today announced financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026.

Grand Canyon Education, Inc. Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results

For the three months ended September 30, 2026:

●Service revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was $267.3 million, an increase of $6.2 million, or 2.4%, as compared to service revenue of $261.1 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. The increase year over year in service revenue was primarily due to an increase in university partner enrollments of 5.2% to 145,270 at September 30, 2026 as compared to 138,073 at September 30, 2025. Revenue from GCU, our largest university partner, for the three months ended September 30, 2026, was earned on tuition and academic-related fees under the Amended MSA, whereas revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2025, was earned under the Original MSA and included, in addition to tuition and academic-related fees, the agreed percentage of ancillary fees related to student housing, food service, athletic ticket sales, arena events, hotel, golf course and apparel operations. Revenue per student slightly decreased between years primarily due to six fewer days of revenue for ground traditional students based on the start date of the Fall semester, resulting in a shift of revenue from the third quarter to the fourth quarter in 2026 which had a $7.5 million impact. Also, we saw a slight decline year over year in revenue per student for online students due to the continued mix shift to students that have a slightly lower net tuition rate. These decreases were offset by service revenue per student for accelerated Bachelor of Science in Nursing (“ABSN”) students at off-campus classroom and laboratory sites generating a significantly higher revenue per student than we earn under our agreement with GCU, as these agreements generally provide us with a higher revenue share percentage, the partners have higher tuition rates than GCU and the majority of our partners’ students take more credits on average per semester.
● GCU enrollments increased to 139,170 at September 30, 2026, an increase of 5.1% over enrollments at September 30, 2025. University partner enrollments at our off-campus classroom and laboratory sites were 7,780, an increase of 12.6% over enrollments at September 30, 2025, which includes 1,680 and 1,325 GCU students at September 30, 2026, and 2025, respectively. Excluding sites that have been closed or are in teach out, total enrollments at our off-campus classroom and laboratory sites increased 14.1% between years. We opened two new sites in the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and closed one site in which we stopped recruiting new students in 2025, thus the total number of sites were 48 at September 30, 2026. Enrollments for GCU ground students were 23,650 at September 30, 2026, down 4.1% from 24,671 at September 30, 2025. GCU online enrollments were 115,520 at September 30, 2026, up from 107,815 at September 30, 2025, an increase of 7.1% between years.
●Operating income for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was $51.5 million, an increase of $33.5 million, or 185.6%, as compared to $18.0 million for the same period in 2025. The operating margin for the three months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025 was 19.3% and 6.9%, respectively. Operating income and operating margin excluding amortization, non-recurring costs and fixed asset disposals for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was $54.7 million and 20.5%, respectively. Operating income and operating margin excluding amortization, litigation settlement, lease termination, impairment and other, severance and fixed asset disposals for the three months ended September 30, 2025 was $58.2 million and 22.3%, respectively.
●Income tax expense for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was $10.7 million, an increase of $5.3 million, or 99.5%, as compared to income tax expense of $5.4 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. Our effective tax rate was 20.0% during the three months ended September 30, 2026 compared to 24.9% during the three months ended September 30, 2025. The increase in income tax expense is due to the increase in income before taxes due to the litigation settlement in the third quarter of 2025, partially offset by a lower effective tax rate. The effective tax rate was favorably impacted by contributions in lieu of state income taxes of $5.0 million for both of the third quarters in 2026 and 2025. The higher effective tax rate in the third quarter of 2025 was due to the nondeductibility of the penalties associated with the litigation settlement.  
●Net income for the three months ended September 30, 2026 was $43.0 million, an increase of $26.7 million, or 164.3% as compared to $16.3 million for the same period in 2025. As adjusted net income was $45.6 million and $49.7 million for the third quarters of 2026 and 2025, respectively.
●Diluted net income per share was $1.67 and $0.58 for the third quarters of 2026 and 2025, respectively. As adjusted diluted net income per share was $1.77 and $1.78 for the third quarters of 2026 and 2025, respectively.

●Adjusted EBITDA decreased 3.2% to $73.5 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $75.9 million for the same period in 2025.

For the nine months ended September 30, 2026:

●Service revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $840.2 million, an increase of $42.2 million, or 5.3%, as compared to service revenue of $798.0 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2025. The increase year over year in service revenue was primarily due to an increase in university partner enrollments of 5.2% to 145,270 at September 30, 2026 as compared to 138,073 at September 30, 2025. Revenue from GCU, our largest university partner, for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 includes revenue for the 3 months ended September 30, 2026 earned under the Amended MSA including only tuition and academic-related fees plus revenue for the six months ended June 30, 2026 earned under the Original MSA including, in addition to tuition and academic-related fees, the agreed percentage of ancillary fees related to student housing, food service, athletic ticket sales, arena events, hotel, golf course and apparel operations. By contrast, service revenue for the nine months ended September 30, 2025 was entirely earned under the Original MSA. Revenue per student stayed flat between years primarily due to the service revenue per student for ABSN students at off-campus classroom and laboratory sites generating a significantly higher revenue per student than we earn under our agreement with GCU, as these agreements generally provide us with a higher revenue share percentage, the partners have higher tuition rates than GCU and the majority of our partners’ students take more credits on average per semester. This increase was offset by six fewer days of revenue for ground traditional students based on the start date of the Fall semester, resulting in a shift of revenue from the third quarter to the fourth quarter in 2026 which had a $7.5 million impact. We also experienced a slight decline year over year in revenue per student for online students due to the continued mix shift to students that have a slightly lower net tuition rate.
●Operating income for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $205.1 million, an increase of $47.3 million, or 29.9%, as compared to $157.8 million for the same period in 2025. The operating margin for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025 was 24.4% and 19.8%, respectively. Operating income and operating margin excluding amortization, non-recurring costs and fixed asset disposals for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $212.5 million and 25.3%, respectively. Operating income and operating margin excluding amortization, litigation settlement, lease termination, impairment and other, severance and fixed asset disposals for the nine months ended September 30, 2025 was $202.3 million and 25.4%, respectively.
●Income tax expense for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $48.9 million, an increase of $10.3 million, or 26.5%, as compared to income tax expense of $38.6 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2025. Our effective tax rate was 22.9% during the nine months ended September 30, 2026 compared to 23.0% during the nine months ended September 30, 2025. The effective tax rate for both of the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025 was favorably impacted by the contribution in lieu of state income taxes of $5.0 million. The effective tax rate stayed relatively flat year over year due to changes in state income taxes, an increase in taxable income and a decrease in excess tax benefits to $1.4 million in the nine months ended September 30, 2026 due to the decline in our stock price as compared to $2.7 million in the nine months ended September 30, 2025. The inclusion of excess tax benefits and deficiencies as a component of our income tax expense increases the volatility within our provision for income taxes as the amount of excess tax benefits or deficiencies from share-based compensation awards are dependent on our stock price at the date the restricted stock awards vest. Our restricted stock awards vest in March each year so any benefit or expense will primarily impact the first quarter each year.
●Net income for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 was $164.2 million, an increase of $34.8 million, or 26.9% as compared to $129.4 million for the same period in 2025. As adjusted net income was $170.0 million and $166.1 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively.
●Diluted net income per share was $6.25 and $4.60 for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively. As adjusted diluted net income per share was $6.47 and $5.90 for the nine months ended September 30, 2026 and 2025, respectively.
●Adjusted EBITDA increased 5.0% to $257.5 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026, compared to $245.3 million for the same period in 2025.

Liquidity and Capital Resources

Our liquidity position, as measured by cash and cash equivalents and investments decreased by $137.9 million between December 31, 2025 and September 30, 2026, which was largely attributable to cash expended for share repurchases

3

and capital expenditures exceeding our cash provided by operations during the nine months ended September 30, 2026. Our unrestricted cash and cash equivalents and investments were $162.1 million and $300.1 million at September 30, 2026 and December 31, 2025, respectively.

Share Repurchase Plan

GCE announced today that on October 28, 2026, the Company’s Board of Directors approved a $355.0 million increase under its existing stock repurchase program, reflecting an aggregate authorization for share repurchases by the Company since the initiation of the program of $2.9 billion. The current expiration date on the repurchase authorization by the Board of Directors is March 1, 2028. As of November 2, 2026, there remained $394.5 million available under the current share repurchase authorization, which includes the increased authorization of $355.0 million. As of November 2, 2026, the Company had 25,510,517 shares of common stock outstanding. The plan permits the Company to make purchases in the open market at prevailing market prices or in privately negotiated transactions in compliance with applicable securities laws and other legal requirements. The level of purchase activity is subject to market conditions and other investment opportunities. The plan does not obligate GCE to acquire any particular amount of common stock and may be suspended or discontinued at any time. The repurchase program may be funded using the Company’s available cash, investments, positive operating cash flows and revolving line of credit.

Adjusted EBITDA  (Non-GAAP Financial Measure)

Adjusted EBITDA is defined as net income plus interest expense, less interest income and other gain (loss) recognized on investments, plus income tax expense, and plus depreciation and amortization (EBITDA), as adjusted for (i) contributions to private Arizona school tuition organizations in lieu of the payment of state income taxes; (ii) share-based compensation; and (iii) unusual charges or gains, such as litigation and regulatory costs, non-recurring costs, impairment charges and asset write-offs, severance costs, and exit or lease termination costs. We present Adjusted EBITDA because we consider it to be an important supplemental measure of our operating performance. We also make certain compensation decisions based, in part, on our operating performance, as measured by Adjusted EBITDA. All of the adjustments made in our calculation of Adjusted EBITDA are adjustments to items that management does not consider to be reflective of our core operating performance. Management considers our core operating performance to be that which can be affected by our managers in any particular period through their management of the resources that affect our underlying revenue and profit generating operations during that period and does not consider the items for which we make adjustments (as listed above) to be reflective of our core performance.

We believe Adjusted EBITDA allows us to compare our current operating results with corresponding historical periods and with the operational performance of other companies in our industry because it does not give effect to potential differences caused by variations in capital structures (affecting relative interest expense, including the impact of write-offs of deferred financing costs when companies refinance their indebtedness), tax positions (such as the impact on periods or companies of changes in effective tax rates or net operating losses), the book amortization of intangibles (affecting relative amortization expense), and other items that we do not consider reflective of underlying operating performance. We also present Adjusted EBITDA because we believe it is frequently used by securities analysts, investors, and other interested parties as a measure of performance.

In evaluating Adjusted EBITDA, investors should be aware that in the future we may incur expenses similar to the adjustments described above. Our presentation of Adjusted EBITDA should not be construed as an inference that our future results will be unaffected by expenses that are unusual, non-routine, or non-recurring. Adjusted EBITDA has limitations as an analytical tool in that, among other things, it does not reflect:

●cash expenditures for capital expenditures or contractual commitments;
●changes in, or cash requirements for, our working capital requirements;
●interest expense, or the cash required to replace assets that are being depreciated or amortized; and
●the impact on our reported results of earnings or charges resulting from the items for which we make adjustments to our EBITDA, as described above and set forth in the table below.

In addition, other companies, including other companies in our industry, may calculate these measures differently than we do, limiting the usefulness of Adjusted EBITDA as a comparative measure. Because of these limitations, Adjusted EBITDA should not be considered as a substitute for net income, operating income, or any other performance measure derived in accordance with and reported under GAAP, or as an alternative to cash flow from operating activities or as a measure of our liquidity. We compensate for these limitations by relying primarily on our GAAP results and only use Adjusted EBITDA as a supplemental performance measure.
